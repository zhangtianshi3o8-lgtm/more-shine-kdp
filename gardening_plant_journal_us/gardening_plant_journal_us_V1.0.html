How to Use This Journal
Your garden's story, told season by season
Why Keep a Garden Journal?
A garden journal is the secret weapon of every great gardener. It captures what you planted, when, where, and how it performed — the details that fade from memory but make all the difference next year. Your journal becomes a personal gardening manual written by experience.
Tips for Better Gardening
1. Know your zone. Your USDA Hardiness Zone determines what will thrive in your climate. Find it at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov, then choose plants suited to your zone for effortless success.
2. Track your seasons. First and last frost dates, planting times, and bloom periods vary every year. Write them down so you can plan earlier and smarter each season.
3. Record what works. Note which varieties thrived and which struggled. Next year's seed orders will be faster, cheaper, and more productive because you remembered.
4. Watch the weather. Temperature, rainfall, and sunlight shape your garden. Your weather notes reveal patterns and help you protect plants before problems hit.
5. Sketch your layout. A simple garden map helps you rotate crops, avoid crowding, and plan succession planting. You will catch mistakes on paper that cost money in soil.
Companion Planting: Some plants grow better together. Basil repels tomato pests, marigolds deter nematodes, and the Three Sisters (corn, beans, squash) support each other beautifully.

Reference USDA Hardiness Zones
USDA Hardiness Zone Guide
Know your zone, grow with confidence
Zone 3
−40 to −35°F
Very short season. Cool-weather crops: kale, spinach, peas, radishes. Start seeds indoors.
Zone 4
−35 to −25°F
Short season. Apples, potatoes, carrots, brassicas. Mulch heavily for winter protection.
Zone 5
−25 to −20°F
Moderate season. Tomatoes, peppers, squash, herbs. Last frost ~mid-May.
Zone 6
−10 to −5°F
Longer season. Most vegetables thrive. Last frost ~late April. Great for perennials.
Zone 7
0 to 5°F
Long growing season. Figs, pecan, many perennials. Last frost ~mid-April.
Zone 8
10 to 15°F
Mild winters. Citrus (outdoor), avocado, olive. Nearly year-round gardening possible.
Zone 9
20 to 25°F
Subtropical. Citrus, bananas, avocados. Very long season. Protect from rare frost.
Zone 10
30 to 35°F
Tropical. Mangoes, papaya, coffee. No freezing temperatures. Year-round growing.
Find your zone at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov. Choose plants rated for your zone or colder for best results.

Reference Seasonal Calendar
Seasonal Planting Calendar
What to do in each season
Spring Tasks (Mar–May)
Prepare beds • Start seeds indoors • Plant cool-season crops (peas, lettuce, spinach) • Transplant seedlings after last frost • Prune fruit trees • Apply mulch • Set up trellises • Divide perennials
Summer Tasks (Jun–Aug)
Water deeply & consistently • Harvest vegetables • Deadhead flowers • Monitor for pests • Stake tall plants • Succession plant • Fertilize heavy feeders • Weed regularly
Fall Tasks (Sep–Nov)
Plant garlic & bulbs • Harvest remaining crops • Clean up garden beds • Compost leaves • Plant cover crops • Protect tender plants • Clean and oil tools • Plan next year's garden
Winter Tasks (Dec–Feb)
Order seeds • Start seeds indoors (late winter) • Plan garden layout • Sharpen and repair tools • Prune dormant trees • Review journal notes • Build new beds • Dream big
