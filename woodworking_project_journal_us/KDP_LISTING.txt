Build It Right, Document Every Cut

The Woodworking Project Journal is the essential companion
for woodworkers, carpenters, and DIY makers. Every project
teaches lessons — dimensions that worked, joints that held,
finishes that looked beautiful. This journal captures that
hard-won knowledge in one organized, professional system.

WHAT'S INSIDE:

40 Project Logs — Each project gets a full two-page spread:
  Left page (Blueprint): Date, status, project name, client,
  overall dimensions (width, depth, height), cut list table
  (part name, wood species, quantity, L x W x T), and a
  dimensioned sketch grid for your design.
  Right page (Build Record): Joinery checklist (butt, miter,
  dado, rabbet, mortise & tenon, dovetail, box joint, biscuit,
  pocket hole, dowel, tongue & groove), primary tools, glue/
  adhesive, finish schedule (type, coats, final grit), cost
  tracking (wood, hardware, hours), quality ratings, and notes.

Reference Section (6 pages):
  • 14 common wood species with Janka hardness, workability,
    price range, and best uses
  • 12 joinery methods explained (from butt joint to through
    dovetail)
  • 9 finishing options with application notes and sanding
    grit progression guide
  • Measurement reference: fraction/decimal/metric conversion,
    lumber dimensions (nominal vs actual), board foot formula
  • 14 workshop safety rules covering personal protection,
    power tool safety, and finishing safety

Shop Management:
  • Project overview inventory (36 entries across 3 pages)
  • Lumber and sheet goods inventory with hardware tracking
  • Tool inventory checklist (power tools, hand tools, jigs)

Reflection & Notes:
  • Year-in-review with best projects, skills, and milestones
  • 2 dot-grid design sketch pads
  • 4 lined notes pages

DETAILS:
  • 104 pages, large 6" x 9" format
  • Professional interior design with oak brown and steel
    blue accents
  • Dot-grid sketch areas for project planning
  • Cream paper, perfect binding
  • Suitable for beginner to advanced woodworkers

Measure twice, cut once. Document everything.
