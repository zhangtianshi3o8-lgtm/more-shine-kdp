What gets measured gets improved
Why Track Your Workouts?
Research consistently shows that people who track their workouts make faster progress, stay motivated longer, and are more likely to reach their fitness goals. A workout log lets you see what works, identify plateaus, ensure progressive overload, and celebrate milestones along the way.
Keys to Progress
1. Progressive overload. Each week, aim to lift slightly more weight, do one more rep, or complete one more set than last time. Small improvements compound into big results.
2. Track everything. Weight, reps, sets, rest times, and how you felt. The more data you have, the better you can optimize.
3. Review weekly. Look back at your last 4-8 workouts. Are you progressing? If not, change a variable — weight, reps, rest, or exercise selection.
4. Track body metrics. Weight, measurements, and photos tell the story the mirror can't. Check monthly for an honest assessment.
5. Set process goals. Focus on showing up and executing, not just outcomes. "Train 4 times this week" beats "lose 10 pounds."
Pro Tip: Take progress photos in the same lighting, same pose, same time of day. Consistency in measurement is everything.
