How to Use This Journal
1
Record each hike as soon as you finish while memories are fresh. Fill in the fields on the left page, then write your story on the right.
2
Use the trail sketch area to draw your route, mark waypoints, or note turn-by-turn directions.
3
Rate your hikes by difficulty and overall enjoyment. Over time you will discover your favorite trails and conditions.
4
Log details about your favorite trails on the next page so you can track which routes suit different seasons and skill levels.
5
Use the summary pages at the back to tally your miles, elevation, and memorable moments at season's end.
6
This journal fits 35 hikes. Carry it in your pack pocket or side pouch.
Pro Tip
Take a moment after each hike to jot down not just the numbers, but how the trail made you feel. Years from now, those notes will be the most valuable part of this journal.

Seven Principles for Outdoor Ethics
1
Plan Ahead and Prepare
Know the regulations and prepare for extreme weather, hazards, and emergencies. Schedule your trip to avoid times of high use.
2
Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces
Use established trails and campsites. Walk single file in the middle of the trail. Keep campsites small and at least 200 feet from water.
3
Dispose of Waste Properly
Pack it in, pack it out. Inspect your campsite for trash or spilled foods. Deposit solid human waste in catholes 6-8 inches deep, 200 feet from water.
4
Leave What You Find
Preserve the past: examine but do not touch cultural or historic structures and artifacts. Leave rocks, plants, and other natural objects as you find them.
5
Minimize Campfire Impacts
Use a lightweight stove for cooking and a candle lantern for light. Where fires are permitted, use established fire rings, fire pans, or mound fires.
6
Respect Wildlife
Observe wildlife from a distance. Never feed animals. Store food securely and keep wildlife wild. Control pets at all times.
7
Be Considerate of Other Visitors
Respect other visitors and protect the quality of their experience. Yield to other users on the trail. Let nature's sounds prevail.

The Ten Essentials
Carry these on every hike, every time
01 Navigation
Map, compass, GPS
02 Sun Protection
Sunglasses, sunscreen, hat
03 Insulation
Extra clothing, jacket
04 Illumination
Headlamp, extra batteries
05 First-Aid Supplies
Bandages, antiseptic, meds
06 Fire
Matches, lighter, fire starter
07 Repair Kit
Knife, duct tape, cordage
08 Nutrition
Extra food, energy bars
09 Hydration
Extra water, filter or tablets
10 Emergency Shelter
Space blanket, tarp, bivy
Safety First
The Ten Essentials form the minimum gear every hiker should carry. Customize your pack for the terrain, weather, and length of your hike.
