How to Use This Journal
1
Record each garden entry as soon as you finish your session while memories are fresh. Fill in the fields on the left page, then write your observations on the right.
2
Use the garden sketch area to draw your bed layout, mark where you planted, or plan where new plants will go.
3
Track your garden through all four seasons. Note the weather, soil moisture, and which tasks you completed each time you visit the garden.
4
Use the companion planting guide to plan which plants grow best side by side, and the planting calendar to know when to sow each crop.
5
At season's end, use the summary pages to tally your harvest, count your gardening days, and set goals for next year.
6
This journal fits 35 garden entries. Keep it with your garden tools so it is always ready for your next visit.
Garden Wisdom
The best fertilizer is the gardener's shadow. Visit your garden often, observe closely, and this journal will become a living record of everything you grow.

Companion Planting Guide
Plants that grow better together
1
Tomato + Basil
Basil repels pests that trouble tomatoes and is said to improve flavor. Plant them as close neighbors.
2
Carrot + Onion
Onions deter carrot flies, and carrots deter onion flies. A classic mutual-protection pairing.
3
Bean + Corn
Beans fix nitrogen in the soil, feeding the tall corn, while corn stalks give beans a natural pole to climb.
4
Cucumber + Radish
Radishes repel cucumber beetles. Sow radishes around cucumbers and let them go to seed as a trap crop.
5
Lettuce + Strawberry
Lettuce grows low and shades strawberry roots, keeping soil cool and moist while deterring weeds.
6
Pepper + Okra
Okra's tall stems provide wind protection for peppers, and peppers help repel some okra pests.
7
Marigold + Tomato
Marigolds repel nematodes in the soil and discourage many common tomato pests with their scent.
8
Nasturtium + Cucumber
Nasturtiums act as a trap crop for aphids and crawling pests, drawing them away from cucumbers.
