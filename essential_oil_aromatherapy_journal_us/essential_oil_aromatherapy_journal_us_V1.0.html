How to Use This Journal
Make every drop count
Why Keep an Oil Journal?
The difference between using essential oils and mastering them is attention. A journal helps you discover which oils work best for you, track your blend recipes so they can be recreated, and build a personal wellness reference over time. Your journal becomes your own aromatherapy apothecary guide.
Tips for Better Blending
1. Start with intention. Before blending, decide your purpose: relaxation, energy, focus, immune support, or something else. A clear intention guides your oil selection and helps you evaluate results.
2. Know your dilution rates. Always dilute essential oils in a carrier oil before applying to skin. A 2% dilution (about 12 drops per ounce of carrier) is standard for adults. See the dilution guide on the next reference page.
3. Record every drop. Write down each oil and the exact number of drops as you blend. This is the only way to recreate a blend you love or adjust one that needs improvement.
4. Label and date everything. Mark each blend with its name, date created, ingredients, and intended use. Oils change over time — some blends improve, others lose potency.
5. Test and observe. After using a blend, note how you felt before and after. Did it help? How quickly? Any reaction? This feedback loop turns trial and error into genuine expertise.
Safety Note: Always perform a patch test before using a new blend on skin. Keep oils away from eyes and sensitive areas. Consult a qualified practitioner for use during pregnancy, with children, or with medical conditions.

Safe ratios for every use
Use Case Dilution Drops per 1 oz Carrier Notes
Perfume / Pulse Points 5% ~30 Small area, short contact
Adult Daily Use 2% ~12 Standard body application
Adult Intensive / Targeted 3-5% 18-30 Short-term, localized area
Face / Sensitive Skin 1% ~6 Dilute extra for face
Children (2+) 0.5-1% 3-6 Use gentle oils only
Bath Dispersed 5-10 Mix with salt/milk first
Diffuser N/A 3-8 Per water reservoir
Massage Oil 2-3% 12-18 Large body area
Common Carrier Oils: Jojoba (closest to skin's sebum, long shelf life) • Sweet Almond (versatile, lightweight) • Fractionated Coconut (non-staining, stable) • Argan (rich, nourishing) • Rosehip Seed (regenerating, face) • Olive (heavy, traditional)
Remember: Less is more with essential oils. Start with fewer drops and increase gradually. A 2% dilution is roughly 12 drops per ounce (30 ml) of carrier oil.

Essential Oil & Aromatherapy Journal 5
Reference Blending Principles
The Art of Blending
Building balanced, harmonious blends
The Note System
Top Notes (first impression): Citrus and mint oils. Light, fresh, evaporate quickly. Examples: Lemon, Bergamot, Peppermint, Sweet Orange. Use 20-30% of your blend.
Middle Notes (the heart): Floral and herbaceous oils. Form the core character. Examples: Lavender, Geranium, Rosemary, Tea Tree. Use 40-60% of your blend.
Base Notes (the anchor): Woody, earthy, and resin oils. Deep, slow to evaporate, long-lasting. Examples: Sandalwood, Vetiver, Patchouli, Cedarwood. Use 10-25% of your blend.
Classic Ratio: 30/50/20
30% top notes • 50% middle notes • 20% base notes. A versatile starting point for any blend. Adjust to taste — some prefer 25/45/30 for a longer-lasting aroma.
Blending Tips
• Start with 3-5 oils. More than that can create a muddy scent profile.
• Let blends rest 24-48 hours before evaluating. Oils need time to integrate.
• Keep a record of every blend — even failures. Learning what does not work is valuable.
• Drop size varies by oil viscosity. Thicker oils (like Vetiver) form larger drops. Count drops, not milliliters, for consistency.
Essential Oil & Aromatherapy Journal 6
Reference Safety First
Safety Guidelines
Enjoy essential oils responsibly
Phototoxic Oils
Citrus oils (especially Bergamot, Lemon, Lime, Bitter Orange) can cause skin sensitivity when exposed to sunlight. Avoid direct sun for 12 hours after application to skin.
Hot Oils
Cinnamon, Clove, Oregano, Thyme, and Cassia are "hot" oils that can cause burning or irritation. Always dilute heavily and patch-test before use.
Pregnancy & Children
Many oils are not recommended during pregnancy or for young children. Consult a qualified aromatherapist or healthcare provider. Generally avoid: Clary Sage, Rosemary, Sage, Wintergreen.
Pet Safety
Tea Tree, Eucalyptus, Pennyroyal, Wintergreen, and Citrus oils can be toxic to pets (especially cats and birds). Research pet-safe oils before diffusing around animals.
Storage
Store essential oils in dark glass bottles away from heat and light. Keep bottles tightly capped to prevent oxidation. Most oils last 2-3 years; citrus oils 1-2 years.
Ingestion
Never ingest essential oils without professional guidance. Even small amounts can cause serious harm. Internal use requires supervision by a qualified practitioner.
Important: This journal is a personal tracking tool and does not provide medical advice. Always consult a healthcare professional for health concerns. Essential oils are complementary, not a replacement for medical treatment.
