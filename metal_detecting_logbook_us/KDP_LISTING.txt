Description:
========================================================
Every Signal Tells a Story

The Metal Detecting Logbook is the essential companion for
detectorists, relic hunters, coin shooters, and beach combers.
From GPS coordinates and detector settings to cleaning methods
and value estimates, this logbook preserves the details that
turn random finds into a personal treasure archive.

WHAT'S INSIDE:

40 Find Logs — Each find gets a full two-page spread:
  Left page (Discovery): Date, time, item name, material,
  estimated age, location data (site name, GPS coordinates,
  terrain type, soil condition), detector settings table
  (model, search mode, sensitivity, discrimination, ground
  balance, coil type), and signal data (VDI number, depth,
  tone type, signal strength).
  Right page (Care & Notes): Cleaning method checklist (none,
  water soak, soft brush, olive oil, chemical, electrolysis,
  professional, left as-found), physical details (dimensions,
  weight, identifying marks, patina/condition), value
  assessment (estimated value, metal weight, melt value),
  significance ratings (rarity, condition), photo reference,
  disposition (kept/returned/sold), and research notes.

Reference Section (5 pages):
  • 12 common metals identification guide with signal strength,
    tone character, and identification tips
  • Cleaning methods for 9 material types with safe/dangerous
    techniques explained
  • Land permission and legal reference (private property,
    public lands, ethical detecting code)
  • Detector settings guide (sensitivity, discrimination,
    ground balance, frequency, VDI number chart)

Site Management:
  • Hunting sites directory (24 entries across 2 pages)
  • Gear inventory checklist (detectors, coils, digging tools)
  • Year-in-review with best finds, highlights, and goals

DETAILS:
  • 100 pages, large 6" x 9" format
  • Professional interior design with copper and verdigris
    accents
  • Comprehensive reference data embedded throughout
  • Cream paper, perfect binding
  • Suitable for beginner to experienced detectorists

Every hole tells a story. The best find is still underground.
